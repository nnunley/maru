# Languages named in the VPRI STEPS reports

A consolidated list of the programming languages that the VPRI STEPS reports (2006 proposal through the 2012 final report) and the related papers describe, name, or use. It is compiled on 2026-11-01 from the notes in this directory only; nothing was fetched. The reports are by Kay, Piumarta, Ingalls, Ohshima, Warth, Amelang, Kaehler, Yamamiya, Freudenberg, Lunzer, Borning, Samimi, Victor, Rose and others; the Maru tree here is Norman Nunley, Jr's rebuild of Maru, not their work. Where the notes do not say something, the cell says "not in the notes".

Page numbers are the printed pages the notes cite ("2007 p.18" means the 2007 first-year report). "Reactive" marks languages the notes describe as dataflow, FRP or time-aware. Grammar file names are in `notes/sources/grammars/` (85 files). "In this tree" was checked by `ls` and `grep` on this worktree, not by running anything, except where a `make check` case from `CLAUDE.md` is cited.

## Named in the reports but not treated as languages

These appear in the notes as frameworks, systems or applications. They are listed so the table above is not read as missing them.

- Gezira: a 2-D vector graphics engine, "written in Nile" (2012); first named 2007 pp.13-15; Amelang. The 2009 note calls it a rendering geometry that Nile expresses. In this tree: the `gezira/` submodule (`gezira/nl/*.nl`, `c/`, `js/`), `libgezira.k`, `test-gezira.k`.
- Jitblt: compositing engine compiled on demand from IS descriptions, 460 lines (2007 p.12; Amelang).
- Lesserphic and Leastphic: GUI frameworks (2008 pp.9-10; Leastphic is one "Box" primitive in under 1000 lines of STEPS code). Lessphic appears in 2012 as Piumarta's work (ref. [11], p.33). The notes do not call them languages.
- KSWorld, LWorld, LBox, DBjr, Frank, Lively Kernel, Light and Lively (a JavaScript replacement in under 200 lines of OMeta and IS, 2008 p.9), XOOS, Source IDE: frameworks, applications or environments.
- Named with no description in the notes: TileScript, Chalkboard, DynaBook Jr, BoldlyCode, OMeta/JS Workspace (2008 pp.11-12, "browser and end-user systems"); the "running parsers backwards" technique (2008 p.12) is a technique, not a language. I could not fill these entries beyond the name and page.
- In this tree but not from the STEPS reports: `irl.g` (irl), `law.g` and `rules.l` (law files and rule engine), `cexpr.g` (C expressions), `maruby.l` (early Ruby, Kurt Stephens' merge), `osdefs.g`, `x86.g`, `arm64.g` (spec readers), `test-dc.g` and `compile-dc.l` (a dc calculator grammar). The notes do not tie these to a STEPS language.
